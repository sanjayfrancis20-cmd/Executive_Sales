Task 4: Internship Interview Questions & Comprehensive Solutions

Curated by Senior BI Analysts for technical viva and dashboard portfolio defense.

1. What are the key elements of a dashboard?

A well-structured business intelligence dashboard contains six fundamental elements:
• KPI Summary Cards / BANs (Big Angry Numbers): High-level quantitative totals (e.g., Total Sales, Net Profit) positioned at the top for immediate executive scanning.
• Time-Series Visualizations: Line or dual-axis charts showing trends, seasonality, and moving averages over time.
• Interactive Slicers / Filters: Controls (Date range, Region, Category) enabling stakeholders to slice and cross-filter data dynamically.
• Categorical & Distribution Charts: Bar charts, waterfall charts, and scatter plots revealing performance drivers and outliers.
• Visual Hierarchy & Consistent Color Palette: Thoughtful semantic colors (e.g., green for profit, red for loss) and 8px grid layout avoiding cognitive overload.
• Navigation & Contextual Metadata: Tab menus, drill-downs, dynamic tooltips, and data refresh stamps.

2. What is a KPI?

A Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a measurable metric evaluated over time against defined strategic targets to assess organizational effectiveness. Unlike simple operational counts, a KPI must be actionable, relevant, and tied to strategic goals. Examples in this dashboard include Total Sales Revenue (top-line volume), Net Profit (bottom-line health), and Profit Margin % (capital efficiency).

3. What are slicers in Power BI?

Slicers in Power BI are visual canvas filters that allow dashboard consumers to filter all other visualizations on the report page simultaneously without navigating backend filter panes. They support dropdown, button/tile, slider, and hierarchy modes. In Tableau, the functional equivalents are Quick Filters, Parameters, and Worksheet Filter Actions.

4. Difference between Power BI and Tableau?

• Data Modeling & Calculation Engine: Power BI uses DAX (Data Analysis Expressions) and tabular data models (VertiPaq engine). Tableau uses Level of Detail (LOD) expressions, Table Calculations, and the Hyper in-memory engine.
• Visual Flexibility: Tableau provides greater granular control over custom visual marks, dual axes, and bespoke statistical charts. Power BI excels in standardized corporate matrix/table visuals and custom AppSource visuals.
• Ecosystem & Cost: Power BI is tightly integrated with Microsoft 365, Azure, and Fabric at a lower entry licensing cost ($10/user/mo). Tableau is deeply embedded in Salesforce with powerful enterprise Tableau Server/Cloud capabilities.

5. How do you make a dashboard interactive?

Interactivity transforms static reports into analytical decision engines via:
• Filter & Parameter Actions: Clicking a region in a map or bar filters all sub-category charts automatically.
• Drill-Down / Drill-Through: Allowing users to click from Year → Quarter → Month or Region → City → Order ID.
• Dynamic Tooltips: Displaying micro-sparklines or contextual diagnostic data on hover (Viz-in-Tooltip).
• Metric Toggle Parameters: Switching axes dynamically between Sales, Profit, and Quantity without cluttering the screen.

6. How do you deal with large datasets in dashboards?

• Pre-Aggregation & Pushdown SQL: Aggregate transactional line items to daily/regional rollups in the database/data warehouse (Snowflake/BigQuery).
• Extracts vs Direct Query: In Tableau, convert Live Connections to compressed .hyper extracts; in Power BI, use Import mode with VertiPaq compression.
• Incremental Refresh: Query only new or modified records rather than rebuilding historical partitions.
• Schema Optimization: Adopt Star Schemas, eliminate high-cardinality unused string columns, and avoid complex row-by-row calculated columns.

7. What chart types do you use for trend analysis?

• Line Charts: The gold standard for continuous time-series, highlighting trajectory, cyclical patterns, and inflection points.
• Dual-Axis Line & Bar Charts: Correlating volume (Sales as bars) against profitability (Profit as lines).
• Area Charts: Emphasizing cumulative volume over time or stacked component shares.
• Moving Average Curves: Smoothing out short-term noise to isolate underlying 7-day or 90-day trajectory.
• Sparklines: Compact micro-lines embedded inside KPI cards for immediate context.
